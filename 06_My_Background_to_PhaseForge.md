# My Background and Research Direction

## Purpose

This note summarizes my actual research background, the small projects I have completed recently, and the research directions I hope to explore in a PhD.

It is written to distinguish clearly between:

- what I have done;
- what I have recently explored;
- what I hope to learn.

## Academic Background

I am completing my B.Sc. in Mechanical Engineering at Khulna University of Engineering and Technology, KUET, Bangladesh, with expected graduation in September 2026.

Current academic standing:

```text
CGPA: 3.81 after the seventh semester
Class rank: 4th out of 130 students
```

My interest in materials research began through an undergraduate Metallurgy course, particularly physical metallurgy and structure-property relationships.

## Undergraduate Thesis

My thesis is:


At this stage, I consider this structure-generation experience rather than a completed grain-boundary research project.

## Machine-Learning Background

I completed a 60-hour Machine Learning with Python course through the EDGE program.

This provided an introductory foundation in machine learning.

I later used Random Forest regression in a small Al-Co learning project.

I do not consider myself an expert in scientific machine learning.

## Recent Computational Thermodynamics Exploration

I became interested in CALPHAD while thinking about phase stability and microstructure modeling.

I have recently used pycalphad for introductory binary phase-equilibrium calculations.

This gave me exposure to:

- using a thermodynamic database;
- calculating equilibrium behavior;
- plotting phase behavior.

I have not performed a complete CALPHAD assessment or database parameterization.

## Recent CHGNet Al-Co Learning Project

I also created a small Al-Co project to become familiar with the idea of an ML interatomic potential.

The project includes:

```text
Al-Co structure generation
        |
        v
pretrained CHGNet
        |
        v
static energy prediction
        |
        v
constrained formation-energy estimate
        |
        v
simple structural descriptors
        |
        v
Random Forest regression
```

The project is intentionally small.

It does not include:

- MLIP training;
- DFT validation;
- rigorous structure sampling;
- full structure relaxation;
- finite-temperature free energies;
- CALPHAD fitting.

## What These Experiences Have Taught Me

My undergraduate work mainly asks:

```text
Given an atomic structure,
how does it behave?
```

My recent thermodynamics exploration has made me interested in another question:

```text
Given composition and conditions,
which phases are favorable?
```

I would like to learn how these two questions can be connected.

## Why the PhaseForge Opportunity Interests Me

The PhaseForge opportunity is relevant to my research development because it combines areas that I currently see as separate pieces:

- computational thermodynamics;
- machine-learning interatomic potentials;
- machine learning;
- phase stability;
- uncertainty-aware modeling;
- automated scientific workflows.

My interest is not based on already knowing all of these methods.

It is based on having a practical atomistic foundation and wanting to learn how to extend it toward thermodynamic phase prediction.

## What I Most Want to Learn

If I enter a PhD in this area, my highest-priority learning goals would be:

1. CALPHAD model development and parameter fitting.
2. MLIP training, validation, and reliable use.
3. Systematic structure sampling for disordered alloys.
4. Phase-stability analysis beyond a few selected configurations.
5. Bayesian uncertainty quantification.
6. Active learning for selecting informative calculations.

In the longer term, I would also like to explore phase-field and microstructure modeling.

## My Research Direction in One Sentence

I want to build on my atomistic simulation experience and learn how atomistic energetics, computational thermodynamics, and machine learning can be combined to study phase stability.
